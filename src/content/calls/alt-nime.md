# Scope and Contributions

alt.nime is a space for contributions that question, challenge, or expand what NIME can be.
Building on the experimental spirit of alt.nime 2026 and the heritage of alt.chi, alt.nime welcomes work that may not fit comfortably within conventional academic categories, not because it is unfinished, but because it deliberately proposes a different way of contributing to NIME. 

alt.nime is a place where it is legitimate to take a position. Submit to alt.nime for situated, critical, speculative, or unconventional contributions that question or expand NIME’s practices, assumptions, or possibilities. We expect contributors to create opportunities for meaningful discussion, a critique or a proposition that tells something about the community itself. Contributions may take any suitable form.
alt.nime welcomes contributions in a wide variety of forms, without privileging any particular format. What matters is not so much the form a contribution takes, but the questions it raises, the perspectives it brings, and the discussions it opens up.

An alt.nime contribution might bring practices, questions, methods, or forms of knowledge from communities and disciplines that rarely appear at NIME. It can also turn the conference itself–its formats, institutions, assumptions, infrastructures, exclusions, or habits–into an object of inquiry or intervention. It may articulate a personal or situated perspective, challenge established research approaches, bring overlooked tensions or disagreements to light, or address issues that the community could collectively reflect upon and act on.

For examples of the diversity of contributions, see[ alt.nime 2026](https://nime.org/alt/).
 

# Submission Requirements

alt.nime contributions are format agnostic. Submissions can be exclusively textual, or take other forms and media. However all alt.nime submissions must include a PDF document using the alt.nime template, and must include the fields:

- **Title, Author Information, Abstract, Ethical Statement, and References.**
- **alt.nime Statement:** Authors must explain why this contribution is relevant to alt.nime and aligns with the expectations for this track, as described earlier in this call. 
- **Presentation Specification:** Authors must describe the format, specification, materials required and describe their intention for how they want to execute or present their proposition during the conference. The final implementation and form will be decided in dialogue with the jury reviewing the work and the alt.nime chairs.

For text-only submissions, the template can be extended beyond the mandatory fields, with a maximum word limit of 6000 words (excluding references and ethical statement).

Supplemental material and/or non-textual submissions may be up to eight A4 pages (or the equivalent, e.g. sixteen A5 or four A2), or in the case of audio-visual submissions, a maximum duration of around 15 minutes.

alt.nime review is non-anonymous. Author information entered in CMT is visible to the jury and chairs because the review process includes direct dialogue among authors, jurors, and chairs.

See [submission instructions](/call/submission-instructions) for more information.

# Review and Selection

For this year, we want to continue developing the “alt.reviewing” process from NIME 2026, and establish a reviewing process through which we aim to foster critical dialogue and collective reflection. Rather than evaluating submissions with conventional academic assessments, the reviewing phase will take form as a curatorial process, where the review is conceived as an opportunity to deeply engage with the ideas raised by submissions.

Each contribution will be reviewed by members of a jury, who are encouraged to formulate situated, constructive, and critical responses. Reviews may take the form of personal perspectives, counterarguments, questions, or reflections that could complement, or expand on the submitted work. Disagreement is welcome, provided it is grounded and contributes to meaningful discussion.

We envision reviewing as the beginning of a conversation rather than its conclusion, so authors and jurors are invited to engage in dialogue during the review period. This dialogue is also intended to extend into the broader NIME community and the conference itself. Where possible, jurors may be invited to take an active role in the presentation and discussion of contributions during the conference, continuing the exchanges initiated during the reviewing process and opening them up to the wider NIME community.

To foster transparency in the curatorial process, the selection and decision-making will be held online, asynchronously, with the chairs, jury, and authors participating. 
The process will:

1. Begin with an initial review by the jury, followed by
2. A period of private, asynchronous discussion between authors, jury and chairs. All parties will have the opportunity to engage in constructive dialogue, exchange perspectives, and discuss the arguments and ideas presented in the submission. 
3. In cases where these discussions are considered valuable for the broader community, and with the authors' and jury’s consent, they will be made accessible through the [NIME forum](https://forum.nime.org), allowing members of the NIME community to contribute their own perspectives and insights to the ongoing conversation.
4. At the end of this discussion period, alt.nime chairs will base their acceptance decisions on the final recommendations of the jurors.

See the call for alt.nime jurors for more details on this process. That call will be linked here once it is published. We invite artists, musicians, researchers and instrument makers from within and beyond academia to respond to the open call for jurors. You can also [sign up to review](/call/reviewing) for NIME 2027 and name alt.nime among the tracks you are willing to take on.

# Presentation and Participation

Accepted contributions will be presented during a conference session and/or space dedicated to alt.nime. The alt.nime chairs are open to unconventional presentation formats and will do their best to accommodate diverse modalities within the practical boundaries of the conference. If an alternative presentation is accepted, authors might have to shape and adapt their work in response to logistical possibilities and constraints.

# Publication and Archiving

Accepted submissions will be published on the conference website and on [NIME.org](https://nime.org/) as part of the alt.nime Proceedings of the International Conference on New Interfaces for Musical Expression.

# Ethics, AI Use, Accessibility, Sustainability, and Inclusion 

All submitted works to NIME 2027 should follow the  **[NIME Principles & Code of Practice on Ethical Research](https://nime.org/ethics/)**

To keep declarations simple and consistent across NIME 2027, please use the [AI Attribution tool](https://aiattribution.github.io/), recently published at CHI and described in this [paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3706598.3713522).

# Additional Considerations with Regard to Other Conference Tracks

Experimental practices are welcome at alt.nime. However, experimentation or novelty alone does not define an alt.nime contribution. What matters is how a contribution uses its experimental approach to raise questions, challenge assumptions, articulate a position, or open up critical discussion within the NIME community.

Work primarily focused on demonstrating, documenting, or sharing an experimental instrument, performance, technique, or process, without this broader dimension of inquiry, may be better suited to the Artifacts & Ideas track.
