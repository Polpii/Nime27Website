# NIME 2027 Calls for Contributions

## Theme: Sensorial Symbiosis

NIME 2027’s theme, Sensorial Symbiosis, explores how musical expression emerges through relationships among performers, instruments, computational systems, environments, and audiences.

How do bodies, instruments, machines, and environments shape one another? What forms of musical expression emerge when sensing, agency, and authorship move between people, materials, and machines? How can interfaces extend, substitute for, or reshape perception, or allow us to encounter traces across time?

Each submission track approaches these questions through its own forms of contribution, presentation, review, and exchange.


## How to Contribute

Whether you’re a researcher, musician, artist, instrument builder, or simply exploring new ways to make music, we’d love to see your contribution at the conference. There are many ways to get involved, grouped around two main deadlines:

Peer-reviewed submissions are due by **23:59 AoE on Sunday, 31 January 2027** (metadata are due one week prior). Submit to **Papers** for research, such as a study of musical gestures, a new instrument design, or a reflection on performance practice; **Music / Performance** for concert works, such as a piece for a custom controller or a human–machine duet; or **Art / Installations** for artworks, such as an interactive sound sculpture or a room that responds musically to visitors.

Curated submissions are due by **23:59 AoE on Sunday, 4 April 2027**. Submit to **alt.nime** for work that fosters critical dialogue about the field and its practices, or challenges established academic formats, conventions, and ways of presenting or sharing knowledge; **Artifacts & Ideas** to share work without an accompanying research paper, such as a DIY instrument presented as a demonstration or a musical-interaction concept presented as a poster; or **Workshops** to organise shared activities, such as exploring collaborative live coding, discussing accessible music-making, or bringing NIME subcommunities together. You can also submit to the **Doctoral Consortium** to receive feedback on a developing PhD project.
